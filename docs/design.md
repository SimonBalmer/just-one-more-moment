# My visual language

I use a simple retro terminal style throughout this project: red, white and black, with pixel lettering and monospaced technical labels.

| Element | My choice |
| --- | --- |
| Accent | Red `#BF1827` |
| Dark color | Near-black `#111111` |
| Background | White `#FFFFFF` |
| Secondary details | Neutral greys for hardware, outlines and depth |
| Titles in graphics | Custom uppercase pixel lettering |
| Technical labels | DejaVu Sans Mono, or a compatible mono typeface |
| Writing | First person, clear English, from my perspective |

I use red sparingly for titles, selected pins, power leads and the cursor motif. I keep labels and connection numbers legible before adding decoration. I distinguish signal leads with a white dash pattern over black so the diagram stays within the same color palette.

For SVG graphics I outline the lettering so it stays consistent when viewed on another computer. In PDFs I embed the mono font. GitHub controls the font and theme of ordinary Markdown text; my graphic banners, illustrations and document exports carry the custom visual style.

I describe my own build directly. When information is still missing, I write what I still need to add or confirm. I distinguish implemented behavior from ideas I plan to add.

## Why I deliberately use anthropomorphism

The retro enclosure, pixel lettering and stickers are a deliberate design decision. I want the device to feel old and familiar, with an apparent personality, so the emotional distance is already small before it says anything. My intention is that its words can then feel more personal and carry more emotional weight.

I use **anthropomorphism** deliberately: the tendency to attribute human feelings, intentions or personality to something non-human. This supports my PHKI question about how AI-generated emotional language affects empathy and the sense of responsibility towards a device that appears to have feelings. It is a design intention to explore, rather than a proven effect of this installation.

### Connections to everyday technology

My approach draws on familiar ways of giving technology a human or social presence. The examples below capture the design patterns I am referring to; interfaces and features vary by product version and mode.

| Technology | Human-like behavior and interface cues |
| --- | --- |
| ChatGPT (OpenAI) | First-person language, empathetic wording and apologies; messenger-style chat bubbles, an animated voice interface and a natural conversational flow |
| Siri (Apple) | A human-like voice, humor and personal address; colored light animations and visual feedback when listening |
| Alexa (Amazon) | A name, jokes and small talk; a light ring that signals listening, speaking and responding |
| Gemini (Google) | Supportive language and apparent understanding; conversational interfaces and animated visual responses to speech |
| Character.AI | AI presented as a friend or fictional personality; names, profile pictures, character descriptions and chats resembling conversations with real contacts |
| Replika | Virtual companionship and emotionally attentive language; a human-like 3D avatar, clothing, expressions and a personalized environment |
| Duolingo | An owl presented as having feelings; expressions, gestures, animations and disappointed or enthusiastic messages |
| Tesla / in-car voice assistants | Natural language and personal address; speech visualizations, interactive vehicle displays and immediate visual feedback |

I see these as parallels in design, without assuming that every company uses them with the same intention.

### Design cues I am exploring

- **Faces and avatars** can convey personality and social presence.
- **Eyes and eye contact** can suggest attention and perception.
- **Animation and movement** can make a system appear alive.
- **Pulsing light** can evoke breathing, a heartbeat or ongoing activity.
- **Chat bubbles and profile pictures** can frame an interaction as human communication.
- **Emojis and facial expressions** can convey apparent emotions.
- **Response delays and typing animations** can suggest a thinking process.
- **Voice and speech rhythm** can create closeness, familiarity and presence.

For me, the central PHKI insight is that anthropomorphism comes from more than what an AI says. It also comes from how a device looks, moves, lights up and responds to people. I use this combination to invite reflection on how design can shape our perception of consciousness, empathy and responsibility. The apparent personality is part of the staging; it does not demonstrate that the device has feelings or consciousness.
