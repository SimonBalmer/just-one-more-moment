# Project concept

## Just One More Moment

I am exploring how **AI-generated emotional language** changes **empathy and the sense of responsibility** for **people interacting with a device that appears to have feelings**. I want my audience to notice **how a simple technical decision can feel like a moral choice, even when the machine may not actually feel anything**.

This raises a broader philosophical question: **How can we recognize or measure genuine consciousness, and how would we know if an AI truly experiences anything?**

## Project question

How does an AI device's technical or emotional language affect visitors' empathy, sense of responsibility and willingness to extend its runtime?

## Situation and audience

A small physical installation presents a countdown, generated text and a button to add time. Visitors choose whether to extend its runtime or let it run out. Local AI is intended to connect the device's words and apparent feelings to the physical object itself.

A connected USB webcam supplies visual context so the device can respond specifically to visitors and their environment. Making that visual input optional through a physical switch is planned.

Visitors are affected by the language and interaction. As the designer, I shape the behavior and the experience through which they encounter the device.

## Main tension

Keeping a machine running may start to feel like caring for a living being. Visitors may feel empathy, guilt or responsibility, even though its emotional language is generated. They control the countdown, while the designer shapes language that may influence their decision.

## Themes

- **Anthropomorphism:** how and why we give human feelings or intentions to a machine.
- **Power and responsibility:** visitors control runtime, while the designer controls the language and interaction.
- **Empathy and consciousness:** whether empathy requires the other side to actually experience feelings.

## Intended reflection

Notice your own emotional reaction to the device. Question whether empathy requires the other side to actually have feelings. Reflect on how language and design can make a technical choice feel like a moral decision.

The installation explores these questions through human interaction; it does not establish or measure machine consciousness.

## Human and AI contributions

Simon's intended contributions include building and assembling the physical device and case, making visual and interaction design choices, testing the experience, observing reactions and writing personal reflections. AI supports coding and generates the live device responses. Detailed implementation credits will be completed with the software.
